## **1.2 Aims of the dissertation project** 

The dissertation project is an integral part of the MSc course, and is an important element in achieving the aims and learning outcomes of the course. The aims of the dissertation project are: 

- To provide a valuable foretaste of what is to be expected in industrial/research environment upon graduation. 

- To equip students with sufficient knowledge and experience to embark on a research career (especially continuing with PhD studies) and in specialised sectors of industry. 

- To apply the knowledge acquired during the taught phase of the programme in practice. 

- To practise problem-solving abilities, project management and report writing skills. 

All MSc students are required to undertake an individual project under the supervision of an academic staff member, and submit a high standard dissertation at the end of the project period. 

The MSc Dissertation Project unit (EEEN60070) is weighted at 60 credits of the 180 credits for the one year MSc programmes, and 120 credits of the 240 credits for the two year MSc programmes. With reference to the _Postgraduate Taught Regulations_ (see the _Programme Handbook_ ): “ _A candidate must pass both the taught element and the dissertation project in order to obtain the MSc degree_ .” Thus, the dissertation project is a vital part of the MSc course. 

## **1.3 Intended learning outcomes** 

The course unit unit aims to: enable, train, and support students to demonstrate an appropriate level of professional competence in the practical development of a suitable application that meets a perceived need, specification, or the realisation of a research project. 

On successful completion of the Dissertation unit, a student will be able to: 

1. Describe and evaluate the methodology used.

# **2 Health, safety, and governance compliance** 

Health and safety is central to the running of the project. All students should have undertaken an introductory health and safety course on Canvas at the time of enrolling in Manchester. Details on this, and the Department’s health and safety policy, are given in the programme handbook. Students should also discuss any specific risks associated with their projects with their supervisors. If further discussion is required students should liaise with the Department’s health and safety officer. 

Before beginning activities students must complete a risk assessment and an assessment of ethical requirements if applicable. A copy of these assessments are to be uploaded at the start of the project (see Section 4.2) and a template risk assessment is provided on Canvas. These assessments should be updated regularly, and as/when needed throughout the course of the project. 

In addition to the above, specific projects may have specific further compliance requirements which need to be addressed, and potentially commented upon in the dissertation report to explain the wider context of the work. Examples include: 

- Health and safety and risk assessment. 

- Chemical use (COSHH). 

- Occupational health surveillance. 

- Use of biological tissues / human tissues / animal by-products (BioCOSHH, human tissues act, animal byproducts regulations). 

- Research ethics (including experiments with humans and animal use). 

- Export controls. 

- Software licensing. 

- Data protection, management, and storage (including GDPR). 

- Access to data sets under license. 

- Image and video copyrights and re-use permissions. 

- Confidentiality and Intellectual Property. 

- Financial management, planning and reporting. 

- Area specific regulations (e.g. medical device documentation, drone use). 

5 

- Following of standard operating procedures for use of kit, maintenance of documentation, and reporting of incidents. 

- Responsible innovation (open science, stakeholder engagement, societal impact assessment/readiness level). 

- Equality, diversity and inclusion (for example fairness and bias in algorithms). 

- Accessibility of materials produced. 

Your project supervisor will discuss which of these apply to your project, and you will then be expected to follow regulatory and University requirements and/or best practice in each of these areas.

# **4 Project period** 

## **4.1 Project work** 

Project work will commence on a full-time basis on Monday 15<sup>th</sup> June 2026, after the Semester 2 examination period. All students are required to work under the supervision of their project supervisor, and must consult their project supervisor on all issues of software, laboratory and, equipment usage. 

**It must be emphasised that students are expected to work on a full-time basis, which is in the UK is an expected 35 hours per week, throughout the entire project period. A student must inform the supervisor if he/she wishes to take time off, such as a vacation.** 

## **4.2 Project outline and compliance assessments** 

At the beginning of the project, all students must prepare a 2-page project outline that describes the scope, motivation, aims and objectives of the project. A brief project plan must also be included. The purpose of the project outline is for students to clearly define the scope of the project and identify what needs to be achieved. It will also allow the supervisors to provide formative feedback on your understanding of the project. No mark will be given on the project outline; only feedback will be provided. 

A health and safety risk assessment is crucial before the project commences as it identifies the health and safety risks involved and methods to reduce those risks. Students must therefore complete a risk assessment at the beginning of the project, to be checked by the supervisor. You should refer to the health and safety induction course to prepare the risk assessments. 

In addition, an assessment of any ethical or other governance aspects of the work must be carried out and documented. See Section 2 for a non-exhaustive list of potential items that may require consideration. Students should discuss any compliance requirements with their supervisor. Students should submit a brief summary of any additional approvals that are required, and suitable evidence that these are in-place, or will be obtained in a suitable time frame. 

Both the project outline and compliance assessments must be submitted to Canvas by 2pm on 1<sup>st</sup> July 2026. They must also be included in the appendix of the dissertation report.

## **4.4 Supervision** 

These guidelines have been further clarified for the 25-26 cohort. 

Throughout the course of the project period (Monday 15<sup>th</sup> June 2026 – 2pm on 1<sup>st</sup> September 2026), supervision meetings are expected to take place on a weekly, in-person basis, and students are expected to attend regularly throughout the project period. An allowance of up to 3 weeks of supervisor absence is included within this expectation. Where valid reasons make this impossible, requests for some meetings to be held online can be considered on a case-by-case basis and require approval by the Departmental Head of Education (DHoE), following submission of the request to the Unit Coordinator. The request must include supervisor approval. Overall, the expectation is that the majority of meetings will take place in person and weekly. Any exception to these arrangements requires explicit prior approval by the DHoE. Supervisors and the Unit Coordinator are not authorised to unilaterally approve these requests. 

Where no explicit departmental approval for remote study has been granted, supervisors must decline requests for online supervision and inform the student that online supervision is not permitted under Departmental policy. If the student does not agree to meet in person within two weeks of that refusal, or continues to request online supervision, the supervisor must escalate the matter to the MSc Programme Director, who will formally restate the requirement for in-person attendance. If the student does not attend an in-person meeting within one week of that escalation, the student is advised to seek an interruption to their studies or to graduate under PGCert/PGDip criteria, as appropriate. 

Academic staff will be engaged in a number of other activities during this period and may also take a period of annual leave for up to three weeks. If your supervisor is away for longer than three weeks, arrangements to cover this absence will be made. It is, however, your responsibility to plan a timetable for your project that takes account of your supervisor’s availability. It is also your responsibility to raise any concerns that you may have with your project or your project supervision at the earliest opportunity with your project supervisor in the first instance, and/or your programme director if necessary.

## **5.2 Dissertation format and presentation** 

These guidelines were updated for the 25-26 cohort. 

The Masters dissertation is a scholarly article that presents an overview of the related background, critically evaluates existing work, and discusses the project work. Therefore, a high and professional standard for the dissertation is expected. 

While the nature of a project varies from one project to another, students are encouraged to consult their project supervisor on the structure and content of the dissertation. 

The University has issued guidelines on the formatting and presentation of an MSc dissertation in the form of a document called: Guidance for the Presentation of Taught Masters Dissertations for UG and PGT Provision. This guidance should be read and followed carefully, apart from with regard to the following criteria where the Department rules, as listed below, must be followed instead: 

- The uploaded report filename must match your student ID number, for example 123456.pdf. 

- The main body of text must not exceed: 

   - 15 pages if on the MSc Robotics. 

   - 35 pages for all other MSc programmes. 

10 

- Dissertations that are over the page limit will be penalised by a deduction of 5 marks due to the violation of the prescribed rules. As a rule, content beyond the page limit will not be awarded marks. However, students are still responsible for all the content they submit. As examples, any content might receive feedback from markers and might be used for disciplinary referrals if there is evidence of academic malpractice. 

- All preliminary pages, abstract, references and appendices are excluded from the page limit. The main body text, included in the page limit, starts from the first content chapter (often entitled “Introduction”) and ends with the last content chapter (often entitled “Conclusions”), 

- Recommended number of words in the main body text. These are guidance values, to set expectations for a producing a high quality report. They are not hard limits. 

   - For a 35-page report, between 8,000 and 12,000. 

   - For a 15-page report, between 3,400 and 5,200. 

- As stated in the Guidance for the Presentation of Taught Masters Dissertations for UG and PGT Provision, the final word count in the main body of text must be inserted at the bottom of the Contents page. 

- The minimum margin is 2 cm throughout the whole dissertation. This applies to left, right, top and bottom margins. 

- The main text of the dissertation should normally be left-justified to aid accessibility and readability. 

- For the main body of text, 1.5 spacing must be used. As stated in the Guidance for the Presentation of Taught Masters Dissertations for UG and PGT Provision, single spacing may be used for quotations, footnotes and references. 

- The font type and size for the dissertations shall be Arial or Arial Nova, size 11. Smaller, size 9, font may be used for figure or table captions. 

- The project outline and risk assessment must be included in the Appendix. 

For L<sup>A</sup> TEX users a template is provided on Overleaf. For Word users a template is provided on Github. You can use other word processing packages, creating your own formatting, if you wish. Even when using one of the supplied templates, responsibility for ensuring compliance with the University of Manchester Presentation of Theses Policy remains with the candidate. Remember that the template is a guide to help you meet the University of Manchester Presentation of Theses Policy. The titles, sections, and content should be adapted by you to meet the needs of your project. 

You are free to upload supplementary materials, such as videos or executable code to support your work. However, you should remember that what is marked is the report describing your work, not the supplementary materials directly. Supplementary items should be used to support the report and, like an Appendix, may not be looked at in detail. For supplementary items which are to be shared publicly we recommend using Github. For items to be shared only with the markers we recommend using the University provided figshare and generating a _private sharing link_ which you can include in your report. Both platforms provide repositories for items where they can be cited and timestamped so that markers can verify they were uploaded before the dissertation deadline.

## **5.8 Plagiarism, collusion, and fabrication or falsification of results** 

As explained clearly in the _Programme Handbook_ , plagiarism, collusion, and fabrication or falsification of results is serious academic malpractice. According to the University’s policy, once an accusation is made of an MSc dissertation, the assessment will be suspended immediately. The case will be reported to the Faculty, who will then form a disciplinary panel to judge on the case. If the accusation is deemed to be serious, the case will be referred to the University level, which has a wider range of penalties available. 

**Students are strongly warned not to take any risk of academic malpractice. Especially with electronic submission of dissertations and anti-plagiarism software it will be discovered and the penalty is severe. In the past, some students have been dismissed from the University due to academic malpractice.** 

We do not give students access to TurnItIn for assessing their dissertation reports prior to submission. Your report is expected to be wholly original to you, apart from clearly cited material, and there should be no need to, or benefit from, checking it in TurnItIn prior to submission. Students are advised to avoid uploading their draft report to plagiarism checking websites as this can lead to the report being added to the database used to check reports against, meaning your final report matches with your draft report. 

Further guidance on plagiarism and other forms of academic malpractice can be found in the University guidance. This includes guidance on the use of artificial intelligence in project work.
